04 / SIMULATE · ONE WINTER DAY, MINUTE BY MINUTE

A day in
Room 12.

Twenty-five pupils, one teacher, 225 cubic metres of air and one child who came to school with a cold. Open the windows, switch on the air cleaners, keep everyone in on a rainy day, then watch what happens to the air, the risk and the heating bill.

Fresh air and clean air are not the same thing.CO₂ tracks how much outdoor air comes in. Infection risk depends on every way particles leave the room, including filtration. Heating cost depends on how much warm air goes out of the window. This page shows all three, minute by minute.

01

CO₂ is a ventilation meter, not an infection meter.

Air cleaners leave CO₂ unchanged but remove infectious particles. A classroom can show high CO₂ and lower infection risk, or the reverse.

02

In winter, fresh air costs heat.

Every litre of outdoor air must be warmed. Opening windows wide can cost several times as much in heating as running two air cleaners. Ventilation still does what filtration cannot: it removes CO₂ and other indoor gases.

03

When the fan runs matters.

Particles left in the air when lessons begin, or that build up on a rainy lunchtime, add to the day's dose. Equal hourly airflow at different times can give different exposures.

Model and assumptions

The room is treated as one well-mixed box, simulated in one-minute steps. Each equation is integrated exactly within a step. The infection probability for a susceptible pupil follows the Wells–Riley form, P = 1 − exp(−dose), where the dose is the pupil's inhalation multiplied by the time-integrated concentration of infectious quanta.
